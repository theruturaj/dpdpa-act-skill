## Phased Enforcement Schedule

G.S.R. 843(E) adopts a structured, three-tiered transition path to allow organizations and the state to build technical, organizational, and legal capacity.

### Phase 1: Institutional & Preliminary Foundation (Effective: 13 November 2025)
*Current Status as of 5 November 2026: **IN FORCE***

Brings into force foundational definitions and powers required to establish the regulatory body and issue secondary standards:
- **Section 1**: Short title and commencement.
- **Section 2**: All definitions (Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, Processor, Consent Manager, SDF, Breach, etc.).
- **Sections 18–26**: Establishment, membership, appointment, qualifications, disqualifications, resignation, removal, and staff of the Data Protection Board of India.
- **Section 30**: Crediting sums realised by way of penalties to the Consolidated Fund of India.
- **Section 34**: Central Government powers to call for information from the Board.
- **Sections 40–41**: General rule-making powers and requirement to lay rules before Parliament.
- **Section 43**: Power of Central Government to issue orders for removal of difficulties (within three years).

### Phase 2: Consent Manager Registration & Architecture (Effective: 13 November 2026)
*Current Status as of 5 November 2026: **COMMENCING SOON (in 8 days)***

Enables the Consent Manager registry, operational guidelines, and early oversight before broad fiduciary duties commence:
- **Section 6(9)**: Registration of Consent Managers with the Board and adherence to technical and operational standards.
- **Section 27(1)(d)**: Jurisdiction of the Board to inquire into breaches by Consent Managers regarding registration conditions or fiduciary obligations.
- **Rule 4 of the DPDP Rules, 2025**: Operational interoperability, security, and verification criteria for Consent Managers.

### Phase 3: Substantive Obligations, Rights, Enforcement, and Penalties (Effective: 13 May 2027)
*Current Status as of 5 November 2026: **FUTURE COMMENCEMENT***

Enforces all operational duties on companies and confers rights upon citizens:
- **Chapter II (Sections 3–10)**: Scope, grounds for processing, notice requirements, valid consent and withdrawal mechanisms, legitimate uses, fiduciary duties (safeguards, breach notices, retention/erasure), children's data restrictions, and Significant Data Fiduciary obligations.
- **Chapter III (Sections 11–15)**: Data Principal rights (access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal, nomination) and Data Principal duties.
- **Chapter IV (Sections 16–17)**: Cross-border transfer rules and statutory exemptions.
- **Chapter V (Sections 27(1)(a)-(c), 28–29, 31–33, 35–39, 42, 44, and the Schedule)**: Full complaints, digital inquiries, TDSAT appeals, mediation, voluntary undertakings, financial penalties up to ₹250 crore, and consequential amendments (TRAI Act, IT Act, RTI Act).
