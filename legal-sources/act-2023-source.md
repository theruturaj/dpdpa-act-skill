# Legal Source: The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

## Document Metadata
- **Official Title**: The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
- **Act Number**: Act No. 22 of 2023
- **Assent Date**: 11 August 2023
- **Publication Reference**: The Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 1, No. 26, New Delhi, Friday, August 11, 2023 / Sravana 20, 1945
- **Administering Ministry**: Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), Government of India
- **Official MeitY PDF URL**: [Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf)
- **Verification Date**: 2026-11-05

## Structure Overview
The Act comprises **44 sections** organized across **5 chapters** and **1 Schedule**:

- **Chapter I: Preliminary (Sections 1–2)**
  - Section 1: Short title and commencement (phased under Section 1(2)).
  - Section 2: Definitions (26 statutory terms, including Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, Data Processor, Child, Consent Manager, Significant Data Fiduciary, Personal Data Breach).
- **Chapter II: Obligations of Data Fiduciary (Sections 3–10)**
  - Section 3: Territorial and material scope.
  - Section 4: Lawful processing grounds.
  - Section 5: Notice requirements.
  - Section 6: Free, specific, informed, unconditional consent; withdrawal; Consent Managers.
  - Section 7: Certain legitimate uses (non-consensual processing).
  - Section 8: General obligations of Data Fiduciaries (security safeguards, breach notice, retention/erasure).
  - Section 9: Specific obligations regarding children (under 18) and persons with disability.
  - Section 10: Significant Data Fiduciaries (DPO, Independent Auditor, DPIA).
- **Chapter III: Rights and Duties of Data Principal (Sections 11–15)**
  - Section 11: Right to access information.
  - Section 12: Right to correction and erasure.
  - Section 13: Right of grievance redressal.
  - Section 14: Right to nominate.
  - Section 15: Duties of Data Principal (penalizable up to ₹10,000 for false/frivolous complaints or misrepresentation).
- **Chapter IV: Special Provisions (Sections 16–17)**
  - Section 16: Cross-border transfer regime (permissible unless restricted by Central Government notification).
  - Section 17: Comprehensive exemptions (judicial, law enforcement, research, foreign contracts, startups, State instrumentalities).
- **Chapter V: Data Protection Board of India (Sections 18–44)**
  - Sections 18–26: Establishment, composition, terms, and digital-by-design infrastructure of the Board.
  - Sections 27–28: Powers, functions, digital inquiry procedure, and interim orders.
  - Sections 29–30: Appeals to TDSAT (60-day period) and crediting of penalties.
  - Sections 31–32: Alternative dispute resolution (mediation) and voluntary undertakings.
  - Sections 33–34: Imposition of financial penalties and factors considered.
  - Sections 35–44: Miscellaneous provisions (good-faith immunity, blocking orders, rule-making powers, Schedule amendments, consequential amendments to TRAI Act, IT Act s.43A omission, and RTI Act s.8(1)(j) substitution).
- **The Schedule: Financial Penalties**
  - Item 1: Security safeguard failure (s.8(5)) – Up to ₹250 crore.
  - Item 2: Breach notification failure (s.8(6)) – Up to ₹200 crore.
  - Item 3: Children's data violation (s.9) – Up to ₹200 crore.
  - Item 4: Significant Data Fiduciary obligation breach (s.10) – Up to ₹150 crore.
  - Item 5: Data Principal duty breach (s.15) – Up to ₹10,000.
  - Item 6: Voluntary undertaking breach (s.32) – Up to the penalty applicable to original breach.
  - Item 7: Any other provision or rule breach – Up to ₹50 crore.
