# DPDP Act 2023 – Cross-Border, Exemptions, Board, Appeals & Penalties (ss.16–44)

Primary Sources:
- Statute: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, Sections 16–44 and the Schedule.
- Subordinate Rules: DPDP Rules, 2025, Rules 13–16 (Transfers, exemptions, inquiry, mediation) and Rules 17–23 (Board functioning, TDSAT appeals, penalty quantification).
- Notifications: G.S.R. 843(E) (Commencement schedule) and G.S.R. 844(E) (Board establishment).

## 2. Cross-Border Personal Data Transfers (Section 16 & Rule 13)
*Commencement Status: Phase 3 (Effective 13 May 2027).*

- **Default Permissibility (Blacklist Model)**: The DPDP Act permits cross-border transfer of personal data to foreign countries/territories by default.
- **Negative List (Blacklist)**: The Central Government may notify countries or territories outside India to which personal data transfer is restricted or prohibited (s.16(1)).
- **Interplay with Stricter Sectoral Laws (s.16(2))**: The DPDP Act does not dilute stricter data localization or transfer mandates in other Indian laws. Any Indian statute or sector-specific regulation providing higher protection or stricter restrictions on transfer (e.g., RBI payment data localization circulars, IRDAI insurance guidelines, SEBI regulations) continues to apply in full force.

## 3. Statutory Exemptions (Section 17 & Rule 14)
*Commencement Status: Phase 3 (Effective 13 May 2027).*

### A. Specific Purpose Exemptions (Section 17(1)):
Exempts processing from Chapter II (Obligations of Data Fiduciaries, except s.8(1) accountability and s.8(5) security safeguards), Chapter III (Data Principal Rights), and Section 16 (Cross-border):
1. **Enforcing Legal Rights**: Processing strictly necessary for enforcing any legal right or claim.
2. **Judicial & Regulatory Bodies**: Processing by any court, tribunal, or regulatory/supervisory authority in India for discharge of regulatory functions.
3. **Offence Investigation**: Prevention, detection, investigation, or prosecution of any offence or cyber incident.
4. **Foreign Contracts (Outsourcing/BPO)**: Processing of personal data of Data Principals not within India pursuant to any contract entered into with any person outside India by any person based in India (crucial for India's IT/BPO export sector).
5. **Court-Approved Mergers & Restructuring**: Processing necessary to carry out a scheme of compromise, arrangement, merger, or amalgamation approved by a court or tribunal.
6. **Loan Defaulters**: Processing to ascertain the financial information, assets, and liabilities of any person who has defaulted in payment of a loan or advance from a financial institution.

### B. State Instrumentalities & Research Exemptions (Section 17(2)):
Exempts specified processing from the **entire Act**:
1. **National Security & Public Order (s.17(2)(a))**: Processing by notified State instrumentalities in the interests of sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, or maintenance of public order.
2. **Research, Archiving, or Statistical Purposes (s.17(2)(b))**: If personal data is processed solely for research, archiving, or statistical purposes, and is not used to take any decision specific to that Data Principal, and conforms to standards prescribed under Rule 14.

### C. Startup & Specific Class Exemptions (Section 17(3)):
- The Central Government may exempt specific classes of Data Fiduciaries, including startups, from Section 5 (Notice), Section 8(3) (Accuracy), Section 8(7) (Erasure), Section 10 (SDF duties), and Section 11 (Access rights).

### D. State Processing Carve-outs (Section 17(4)):
- For processing by the State or its instrumentalities, Section 8(7) (Erasure) and Section 12(3) (Right to erasure) do not apply, and Section 12(2) does not apply where no decision affecting the Principal is made.

### E. 5-Year Transitional Relief (Section 17(5)):
- For a period of five years from the commencement of the Act, the Central Government may notify that any provision shall not apply to such Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries for a specified period.

## 4. The Data Protection Board of India (Sections 18–28)
*Established 13 November 2025 via G.S.R. 844(E) under Section 18.*

- **Nature & Seat**: Body corporate with perpetual succession; principal headquarters at New Delhi (NCR); digital-by-design institution conducting digital proceedings.
- **Composition (s.19)**: Chairperson and Members appointed by the Central Government for a 2-year term (re-appointable); at least one member with legal expertise.
- **Independence & Restrictions (ss.21–22)**: Strict disqualifications (insolvency, conviction, conflicting interest); removal only after an inquiry; 1-year post-service employment bar.
- **Powers and Functions (s.27)**:
  - Inquire into personal data breaches upon intimation (s.27(1)(a)).
  - Inquire into complaints lodged by Data Principals (s.27(1)(b)).
  - Inquire upon references made by Central/State Governments or directions of a court (s.27(1)(c)).
  - Inquire into Consent Manager registration violations (s.27(1)(d) – commencing 13 November 2026).
  - Issue binding directions and impose financial penalties.
- **Procedure & Civil Court Powers (s.28)**: Natural justice principles; digital filings; powers of a civil court to summon witnesses, receive evidence on affidavit, and inspect documents.

## 5. Alternative Dispute Resolution & Appeals (Sections 29–32)

1. **Mediation (Section 31 & Rule 16)**: The Board may, if it considers it appropriate, refer parties to mediation.
2. **Voluntary Undertaking (Section 32 & Rule 16)**:
   - The Board may accept a voluntary undertaking from an entity facing inquiry.
   - Acceptance operates as a complete bar on proceedings for matters covered by the undertaking.
   - Breach of a voluntary undertaking constitutes a breach of the Act and is subject to full financial penalties.
3. **Appeals to TDSAT (Section 29, Section 30 & Rule 22)**:
   - Any person aggrieved by an order or direction of the Board may appeal to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) under Section 29.
   - **Limitation Period**: **60 days** from receipt of the Board's order.
   - The Tribunal shall endeavor to dispose of the appeal finally within **six months**.
   - Appeals against TDSAT orders lie before the Supreme Court of India under Section 18 of the TRAI Act, 1997.

## 6. Financial Penalties (Section 33 & The Schedule)
*Commencement Status: Phase 3 (Effective 13 May 2027).*

Penalties are adjudicated by the Board considering:
- Nature, gravity, and duration of the breach.
- Type and sensitivity of personal data affected.
- Repetitive nature of the breach.
- Gain realized or loss avoided.
- Mitigating steps taken.
- Proportionality and deterrence.

### The Penalty Schedule (Maximum Limits):

| Statutory Breach | Controlling Section | Maximum Financial Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **Failure to take reasonable security safeguards** to prevent personal data breach | Section 8(5) | **₹250 crore** |
| **Failure to notify Board and affected Data Principals** of a personal data breach | Section 8(6) | **₹200 crore** |
| **Breach of obligations regarding children** or persons with disability | Section 9 | **₹200 crore** |
| **Breach of additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciaries** | Section 10 | **₹150 crore** |
| **Breach of duties by a Data Principal** | Section 15 | **₹10,000** |
| **Breach of terms of a voluntary undertaking** | Section 32 | Up to the maximum penalty applicable to the underlying breach |
| **Any other breach of provisions of the Act or Rules** | Residual | **₹50 crore** |

*Note (Section 42)*: The Central Government may amend the Schedule by notification, but cannot raise any penalty limit to more than twice the amount specified. All penalties are credited to the Consolidated Fund of India (s.30).

## 7. Miscellaneous Provisions & Consequential Amendments

- **Protection of Good Faith Action (s.35)**: Suit immunity for Central Government, Board, Chairperson, and Members.
- **Power to Call for Information (s.36)**: Central Government may call for information from Board, Fiduciaries, and Intermediaries.
- **Power to Block Access (s.37)**: After two or more penalties on a Fiduciary, upon reference from the Board, the Central Government may direct blocking of public access to its platform/service.
- **Bar of Jurisdiction (s.39)**: Civil courts are expressly barred from entertaining any suit or proceeding regarding matters within the Board's remit.
- **Consequential Amendments (s.44)**:
  - *TRAI Act, 1997*: Expands TDSAT jurisdiction to hear DPDP appeals.
  - *Information Technology Act, 2000*: **Omits Section 43A** (SPDI Rules) and related provisions.
  - *Right to Information Act, 2005*: Replaces Section 8(1)(j) with blanket exemption: *"information which relates to personal information"*.
